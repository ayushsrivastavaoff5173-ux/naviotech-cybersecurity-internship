============================================================
PHISHING SIMULATION TESTING & EXECUTION GUIDE
(Step-by-Step Instructions)
============================================================

OVERVIEW
This guide walks you through running an ethical, consented phishing awareness 
simulation. Follow each step carefully to ensure ethical compliance and valid results.

Timeline: 10 days total
Effort: ~20-30 hours (planning, execution, analysis, training)
Participants: 40-50 recommended (larger = more statistical confidence)

---

PHASE 1: PLANNING & PREPARATION (Days -7 to -1)

[ ] STEP 1: Get organizational approval
    • Meet with IT Security Manager and Compliance Officer
    • Explain the study goals, methodology, and ethical safeguards
    • Obtain written approval before proceeding
    • Document approval in project records

[ ] STEP 2: Select participant list
    • Work with HR/IT to identify test group (recommend: mixed departments, 
      ~40-50 people)
    • Avoid: C-suite, external contractors (unless specific permission)
    • Segmenting by department is useful for identifying high-risk groups
    • Example groups:
      - Finance department (8 people)
      - IT/Security (6 people)
      - HR (9 people)
      - Operations (12 people)
      - Sales (7 people)
      - Others (remaining staff)

[ ] STEP 3: Prepare materials
    ✓ Phishing email template (see: phishing-email-template.txt)
    ✓ Google Form / fake login page (see: google-forms-setup-guide.txt)
    ✓ Consent form (see: consent-form-template.txt)
    ✓ Training materials for follow-up (links to resources, videos, etc.)
    ✓ Communications plan (email sequence, timeline, talking points)

[ ] STEP 4: Set up data tracking
    • Create a spreadsheet to manually track:
      - Participant email address
      - Date consented
      - Email send date/time
      - Open timestamp (if trackable)
      - Click timestamp
      - Form submission timestamp
      - Department
      - Any manual reports to IT
    • Set up Google Forms response collection (automatic timestamp logging)
    • Configure email campaign tracking (if using email marketing tool like Mailchimp)

[ ] STEP 5: Create communications timeline
    • Draft all emails you'll send:
      1. Consent request email (Day 0)
      2. Phishing simulation email (Day 1)
      3. Reminder email (Day 3, if low engagement)
      4. All-clear email (Day 8, revealing the test)
      5. Training resources email (Day 9)
    • Get approval from IT/Compliance before sending

---

PHASE 2: CONSENT COLLECTION (Day 0)

[ ] STEP 6: Send consent request
    Template email:
    
    Subject: Security Awareness Study — Voluntary Participation
    Body:
    Dear Team,
    
    We are conducting a security awareness study to improve our organization's
    resilience to phishing attacks. This is a voluntary, educational training 
    exercise.
    
    If you agree to participate, you may receive a test phishing email and be 
    asked to complete a form. No real data will be compromised.
    
    Attached: Informed Consent Form
    
    To consent, please:
    1. Read the attached consent form carefully
    2. Print and sign it
    3. Return it to [EMAIL] by [DATE]
    
    Questions? Contact [TRAINER NAME] at [EMAIL]
    
    Thank you for your commitment to cybersecurity!

[ ] STEP 7: Collect signed consent forms
    • Set deadline: typically 24-48 hours after consent email
    • Keep a master list of who has consented
    • Only send phishing simulation email to consented participants
    • Document who declined to participate (and exclude from simulation)

[ ] STEP 8: Brief IT/help desk (OPTIONAL but RECOMMENDED)
    • Inform IT Support that a phishing simulation is happening
    • If employees report the test email to IT, support should:
      ✓ Acknowledge it's a training exercise (if email/form makes it clear)
      ✓ Not flag it as a security incident
      ✓ Redirect reporter to training coordinator
    • Prepares IT for potential questions/reports

---

PHASE 3: PHISHING SIMULATION (Days 1-7)

[ ] STEP 9: Send phishing simulation email (Day 1)
    • Send from spoofed/test address: support@company-internal.test
    • Subject: "Action Required: Password Expiration Notice (24 hours)"
    • Body: See phishing-email-template.txt
    • Include link to Google Form (fake login page)
    • Send at time when people are likely to be checking email 
      (e.g., 9-10 AM on a Tuesday/Wednesday)
    • Log exact send time and recipients

[ ] STEP 10: Monitor responses (Days 2-7)
    Daily tracking:
    • Check Google Forms for new submissions (timestamp, IP)
    • Monitor email platform for open rates / click rates (if available)
    • Track any manual reports to IT (note: encourage reporting!)
    • Keep tally in your tracking spreadsheet
    
    What to look for:
    • Email opens: ~70-90% typical
    • Link clicks: ~40-70% typical
    • Form submissions: ~20-50% typical
    • Reports to IT: ~5-10% (these are "security champions" — note for follow-up)

[ ] STEP 11: Send reminder email (Day 3 — OPTIONAL)
    If you want more data/engagement, send a follow-up reminder:
    
    Subject: Friendly Reminder: Password Expiration (24 hours)
    Body: Similar to original phishing email
    
    (Sends should show different email addresses if possible, to test if 
     people notice the pattern — e.g., first from "support@...", second from 
     "noreply@...")

[ ] STEP 12: Stop the simulation (Day 7-8)
    • Stop sending follow-ups
    • Prepare all-clear message
    • Export Google Forms responses to spreadsheet
    • Tally results in your tracking sheet
    • Identify who needs training (anyone who clicked or submitted)

---

PHASE 4: REVEAL & TRAINING (Days 8-10)

[ ] STEP 13: Send all-clear email (Day 8)
    Subject: Security Awareness Study Complete — Training Exercise Revealed
    Body:
    
    Dear Team,
    
    The phishing email you may have received on [DATE] was a TRAINING EXERCISE.
    
    This was NOT a real security threat. No employee credentials have been 
    compromised or misused.
    
    If you clicked the link or entered credentials:
      ✓ You are NOT in trouble
      ✓ This is an opportunity to learn
      ✓ Phishing is effective because it exploits normal human trust
      ✓ With awareness, you can avoid it in the future
    
    TRAINING SESSION (MANDATORY for anyone who fell for the test):
    Date: [DATE]
    Time: [TIME]
    Link: [ZOOM/TEAMS/IN-PERSON]
    
    Topics:
    • How phishing works
    • How to spot red flags (sender spoofing, urgency, suspicious links)
    • What to do if you receive a suspicious email
    • Reporting procedures
    
    OPTIONAL RESOURCES (for everyone):
    • [Link to phishing awareness video]
    • [Link to email security best practices]
    • [Link to password manager setup guide]
    
    Questions? Contact [TRAINER] at [EMAIL]
    
    Thank you for your participation!

[ ] STEP 14: Compile results
    Create a summary table:
    
    Metric                    | Count  | Percentage
    ---|---|---
    Total Participants        | 42     | 100%
    Emails Opened             | 38     | 90.5%
    Links Clicked             | 28     | 66.7%
    Form Submissions          | 18     | 42.9%
    Reported to IT            | 4      | 9.5%
    No Interaction            | 4      | 9.5%
    
    By Department:
    Department | Staff | Clicks | % | Risk Level
    ---|---|---|---|---
    Finance    | 8     | 2      | 25%   | HIGH AWARENESS
    IT/Sec     | 6     | 0      | 0%    | EXCELLENT
    HR         | 9     | 4      | 44%   | MODERATE
    Operations | 12    | 11     | 92%   | HIGH RISK
    Sales      | 7     | 11     | 100%  | CRITICAL

[ ] STEP 15: Provide training (Days 9-10)
    MANDATORY for anyone who clicked or submitted:
    • Duration: 30-45 minutes
    • Format: Live session (can be recorded)
    • Content:
      1. Phishing psychology (why it works)
      2. Common techniques (urgency, spoofing, credential harvesting)
      3. Red flag checklist (sender, urgency language, links, attachments)
      4. Reporting procedure
      5. Q&A
    
    OPTIONAL for everyone:
    • Recorded training session (if live wasn't attended)
    • Self-paced email security course
    • Password manager setup guide
    • Two-factor authentication implementation guide

[ ] STEP 16: Document lessons learned
    For your report, include:
    • What went well
    • What could be improved next time
    • Feedback from participants (optional survey)
    • Recommended follow-up actions:
      - Department-specific training (especially Sales, Operations)
      - Implement technical controls (MFA, email filtering)
      - Repeat simulation in 3-6 months
      - Build security champion program (recognize the 9.5% who reported)

---

POST-SIMULATION (Week 2+)

[ ] STEP 17: Follow-up survey (optional)
    Send a brief survey to participants:
    • Was the training helpful?
    • What did you learn?
    • Would you like more training?
    
    Use feedback to improve future simulations.

[ ] STEP 18: Schedule repeat simulation
    Best practice: Run quarterly simulations (every 3 months)
    • Phishing awareness degrades over time without reinforcement
    • Quarterly keeps the message fresh
    • Track improvement over time
    • Adjust difficulty level (make future ones slightly harder)

[ ] STEP 19: Implement technical controls (recommendation)
    In parallel with training:
    • SPF/DKIM/DMARC email authentication (prevents domain spoofing)
    • Email content filtering (blocks known phishing domains)
    • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for all critical accounts
    • Browser security awareness extensions
    • Web content filtering (blocks known malicious domains)

---

TROUBLESHOOTING

Q: "What if someone reports the simulation email to the help desk?"
A: Have prepared IT to respond: "This is a security awareness training exercise. 
   Please see the all-clear email for details. Contact [TRAINER] if questions."

Q: "What if someone gets really upset or feels embarrassed?"
A: Reinforce that this is training, not punishment. Offer 1-on-1 support.
   Emphasize: "Phishing is effective because it exploits normal human trust."

Q: "What if I want to do this again?"
A: Repeat every 3-6 months. Track metrics over time to show improvement.
   Adjust difficulty/content to keep it fresh.

Q: "Can we use a tool like Gophish instead of Google Forms?"
A: Yes, more advanced tools (Gophish, KnowBe4, Phishme) automate tracking and 
   training. Google Forms is simpler and compliant for a student project.

ETHICS CHECKLIST (Before You Start!)

[ ] Written approval from IT Security Manager
[ ] Written approval from Compliance Officer
[ ] Consent forms signed by ALL participants before simulation begins
[ ] Phishing email clearly labeled as training in follow-up messages
[ ] No real credentials or sensitive data collected
[ ] Aggregate/anonymize results before sharing
[ ] Training provided to anyone who fell for the simulation
[ ] No punitive action taken
[ ] Participant can opt out at any time

If you can't check all boxes, DO NOT PROCEED with the simulation.
Ethics is non-negotiable.
