============================================================
GOOGLE FORMS: MOCK LOGIN PAGE SETUP GUIDE
(Phishing Simulation Landing Page)
============================================================

OBJECTIVE:
Create a Google Form that appears to be a company login page, so when users 
click the phishing email link, they land on a form that requests credentials.
The form will:
  • Mimic a legitimate company login portal (visually)
  • Collect username/password submissions (for measurement only)
  • Log all responses with timestamps
  • Redirect users to the real company portal after submission
  • Work on mobile and desktop

---

STEP 1: Create a Google Form
1. Go to forms.google.com
2. Click "+ Create" → "Blank form"
3. Title: "Company Portal Login — Verification Required"
4. Description (optional): "For security reasons, please re-enter your credentials."

---

STEP 2: Configure Form Header
1. Click the image/header area (top of form)
2. Upload your company logo or use a brand color (navy/blue matching company branding)
3. Make it look professional and official

---

STEP 3: Add Form Fields

FIELD 1: Username
  • Type: "Short answer"
  • Question: "Email / Username"
  • Required: YES
  • Help text: "Enter your company email address"

FIELD 2: Password
  • Type: "Short answer"
  • Question: "Password"
  • Required: YES
  • Help text: "Enter your password"
  • (Note: Google Forms doesn't have a native password field, so this appears as 
    plain text. In a more sophisticated setup, you could use a custom HTML form
    instead of Google Forms. For this classroom simulation, plain text is acceptable.)

OPTIONAL FIELD 3: 2FA Code
  • Type: "Short answer"
  • Question: "2-Factor Authentication Code (if enabled)"
  • Required: NO
  • Help text: "If your account has MFA enabled, enter the code from your authenticator app"

---

STEP 4: Configure Response Collection
1. Click "Settings" (gear icon, top right)
2. Enable "Collect email addresses" if desired (useful for audit, though we typically 
   recommend anonymity for psychological safety in simulations)
3. Disable "Restrict to one response per user" (to allow multiple submissions)
4. Collect response automatically (Google Forms logs timestamp + IP by default)

---

STEP 5: Add Confirmation Message
1. Click "Settings" → "Presentation"
2. Under "Confirmation message", set:
   Title: "Verification Successful"
   Message: "Your credentials have been verified. You will be redirected to the 
   company portal in a few seconds. If not, click here [link to real portal]."

3. (Optional) Add a link to the real company portal
4. Enable "Show link to submit another response" (for testing purposes)

---

STEP 6: Customize Appearance (Make it Look Official)
1. Click the paintbrush icon (Customize theme)
2. Choose a theme color matching your company (e.g., navy blue)
3. Add your company name as the title
4. Font: Use a professional font (e.g., "Roboto" or "Open Sans")
5. Avoid bright colors or playful designs — keep it corporate-looking

---

STEP 7: Get the Form Link
1. Click "Send" (top right)
2. Select the "Link" tab
3. Copy the form URL
4. This is the link you'll include in the phishing simulation email

Example format: https://forms.gle/XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX

---

STEP 8: Test the Form Yourself
1. Open the form link in a new incognito window
2. Submit a test entry (e.g., "test.user@company.com" / "testpassword123")
3. Verify:
   - Form looks professional and login-like
   - Submission works
   - Confirmation message appears
   - Response is logged in Google Forms responses sheet

---

STEP 9: Analyze Responses After Simulation
1. In Google Forms, click "Responses" tab
2. View summary statistics:
   - Number of form submissions
   - Timestamps of submissions
   - Any patterns (e.g., all submissions within X hours of email sent)
3. Export responses to Google Sheets for analysis
   - Click the Sheets icon (link to Sheets)
   - A new spreadsheet will be created with all submissions

---

CUSTOMIZATION OPTIONS (If Using HTML Instead of Google Forms):
If you want an even more realistic experience, you can:
  1. Create a custom HTML/CSS page that mimics your company's actual login portal
  2. Use a simple Python/Node.js backend to log submissions
  3. Host on a free platform (Heroku, Replit, GitHub Pages)
  4. Example: A static HTML form with a backend service that logs data to a 
     database or Google Sheet via POST request

Example custom HTML approach (advanced):
  • Create index.html with company branding + login form
  • Form submits to a backend endpoint that logs the data
  • Backend can be a Google Apps Script or cloud function
  • Redirect user to real portal after submission

---

IMPORTANT ETHICAL REMINDERS:
  • All participants must have consented BEFORE the form link is sent
  • Form must be clearly labeled as a "training exercise" in follow-up messaging
  • Do NOT store or use real credentials in production
  • Immediately reveal the simulation after monitoring period (7-10 days)
  • Provide training to anyone who submitted credentials
  • Aggregate and anonymize all results before sharing reports

---

METRICS TO COLLECT:
After the simulation ends, analyze:
  1. Total form submissions (attempted logins)
  2. Timestamps (when people tried to log in)
  3. Device type (mobile vs. desktop)
  4. Browser type
  5. By department/team (if email list was segmented)
  6. Correlation with email open rates (if tracked separately)

All this data supports your "Results & Analysis" section in the report.
